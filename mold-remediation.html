Mold Remediation After Water Damage

Mold often follows unresolved moisture. If you have musty odors, visible growth, or a recent leak, we can help connect you with independent mold remediation providers.

Mold needs moisture and a food source, and drywall paper, dust, wood, and many household materials qualify. In a dry region like central New Mexico, outdoor humidity is often low, but indoor leaks, poor drying after a flood, and evaporative cooler problems still create mold inside homes. Our referral service connects callers who need mold remediation help, especially when it is tied to water damage.

When mold concerns usually show up

Homeowners often call after noticing a persistent musty smell, spotting discoloration on walls or ceilings, or finding growth behind a recently wet baseboard. Others are dealing with a known leak that sat for days or weeks. Not every stain is mold, and not every musty smell means the same scope of work, so a proper assessment matters before tear-out begins.

Local moisture sources to check

  • Swamp cooler overflows, leaky ducts, or wet pads dripping into the attic
  • Ceilings and attics after a monsoon roof leak
  • Flooring and baseboards after a slab leak or burst pipe
  • Walls and floors that were not fully dried after flooding
  • Bathrooms without a working exhaust fan

What mold remediation generally involves

Independent mold remediation providers typically address the moisture source first (or coordinate with whoever is fixing it), isolate work areas when appropriate, remove or clean affected materials, and dry the space. Heavily affected porous materials are often thrown out rather than cleaned. The right approach depends on the size of the area, the materials involved, and how sensitive the people living there are.

  • Confirm moisture is stopped or controlled
  • Assess visible growth and hidden cavities when needed
  • Contain dust during removal when the job calls for it
  • Clean and treat surfaces using the provider’s methods
  • Verify the area is dry before closing walls back up

What we will not claim

We do not diagnose health conditions, guarantee “mold-free” homes, or promise laboratory results. We also do not pretend to be a remediation contractor. If you have medical concerns, speak with a qualified health professional. For the home itself, ask the provider about their process, training (for example, IICRC), and how they will handle your specific rooms.

Prevention tips after a leak

  1. Dry wet materials as promptly as safety allows.
  2. Run ventilation in bathrooms and kitchens.
  3. Watch evaporative cooler drains and pads for overflow, and drain the cooler for winter.
  4. Fix plumbing drips instead of leaving a bucket under them.
  5. After storms, check ceilings and closets for new stains.

Quick answers

How big an area needs a professional?

U.S. EPA guidance suggests professional help for areas larger than about 10 square feet, for mold in HVAC systems, or for mold tied to sewage or contaminated water.

Is bleach enough?

It may help on small areas of hard surfaces, but it does not fix the moisture problem and is not recommended for porous materials like drywall.
